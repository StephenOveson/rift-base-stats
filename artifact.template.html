How the numbers are derived

A stat at level L is base + growth × (L−1) × (0.7025 + 0.0175 × (L−1)) — Riot's per-level curve. The bracket reaches exactly 1.0 at level 18, so a maxed champion is precisely base + 17 × growth; every level before that is worth less than a flat share (at level 10 the multiplier is 7.74, not 9), which is why early levels feel thinner than the raw growth number suggests. Attack speed is the exception: its growth is a percentage applied to the base, so it compounds as base × (1 + growth% × curve).

BST is a base stat total: each of the seven stats is min–max scaled from 0 to 100 across the whole roster, then averaged. It is a shape comparison, not a power ranking — it weights move speed as heavily as health, and knows nothing about abilities. Move the level slider and it recomputes, so a champion with a fat base and thin growth visibly slides down the board as you climb.

The Base / At level / Growth switch reinterprets all seven stat columns at once: values at level 1, values scaled to the chosen level, or the per-level growth rate. BST recomputes with them, so in the growth view it ranks who scales hardest rather than who is strongest now. A stat that is identical across the whole roster carries no information in a given view and is dropped from that average rather than adding the same constant to everyone — which is why move speed, having no growth at all, sits out the growth view and shows a dash.
